Then water mitigation dries the structure, water damage cleanup takes out what cannot be saved, and water damage repair rebuilds the room. Large storm and sewage events fall under flood damage restoration, and growth that followed a leak is handled through mold remediation.

First hour

What to do while you wait for help

The first hours decide how big the job becomes. Water moves into baseboards, wicks up drywall and soaks pad in a matter of hours, so what you do before the crew arrives matters.

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve or the main shutoff. If you are not sure where it is, your water bill or the city provider map shows who to call.

Make it safe

Keep people and pets out of the wet area. Do not touch outlets or the panel if water is near them, and call your utility if gas or power is a risk.

Lift what you can

Move small furniture, rugs and electronics to dry ground. Leave heavy items alone, since lifting them wet can hurt you.

Photograph and call

Take wide and close photos of the water, the source and the damaged items. Then call the line and describe what is wet.

Process

How a job runs from first call to finished room

A water damage job follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard as the industry reference, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. The drying side is explained on the water mitigation page, and the rebuild is covered under water damage repair.

Step 01

Call and describe the water

Tell us where it started, what is wet and how long it has been. That sets the first moves.

Step 02

Find where the water went

Moisture meters and a thermal camera show wet zones behind paint, under floors and inside ceilings.

Step 03

Scope and price explained

You hear what must be removed, what can be dried and what the work is expected to cost before it begins.

Step 04

Extract, remove and dry

Standing water comes out, ruined material is bagged, and air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure.

Step 05

Verify and rebuild

Readings confirm dry goals are met. Then drywall, flooring and paint are rebuilt, with mold handled first if it showed up.

Cost and insurance

What drives the price and what helps a claim

No honest company can name a number from a phone description alone. The price of a water damage job depends on the water category, the square footage affected, the materials involved, the days of drying and how much rebuild is left. Scope and price are explained before work starts, so the decision stays with you.

Insurance is a common question. In general terms, sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep photos, a written scope and the drying log, since those support any claim.

Warning signs

Signs water damage has already set in

Not every job starts with a flood. Many callers find a stain, a smell or a soft spot and wonder how serious it is. These are the signs we hear about most, and each one has a service page that explains the next step, from water damage repair to mold remediation.

A ceiling stain that grows

A brown ring that spreads or sags means water is still collecting above. Keep people clear and call before the board gives way.

Walls that feel cool or soft

Drywall that gives under a thumb or paint that bubbles has absorbed water from behind, often from a pipe or a roof leak.

Floors that cup or lift

Hardwood and laminate show moisture early. Edges that curl or boards that lift point to water under the surface.

A musty smell with no visible water

Odor is a late sign that damp material has been sitting for days. A reading behind the wall finds the source.

Local detail

Water damage in Newbury Park neighborhoods

Newbury Park is not one kind of house. Casa Conejo was the first planned community here, Ventu Park began as a 1940s development, and Dos Vientos Ranch and Rancho Conejo Village are the newer master-planned areas. The Rancho Conejo area to the north is mostly industrial and office space. Older plumbing and newer plumbing fail in different ways, so we check readings behind the wall instead of judging by paint.

Arroyo Conejo, also called Conejo Creek, runs southwest through town. Look up your address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center and read your policy before winter. The area around Newbury Gateway Park, the Stagecoach Inn and the trails of Wildwood Regional Park north of town is the part of the valley many families call home, and roof and window leaks tend to show up in the rainy season.

Most of Newbury Park sits inside the City of Thousand Oaks. Four water providers serve the Conejo Valley, so use the city's Find Your Water Provider map or check your bill to learn who handles the meter at your street. For permit questions, the city building division is at (805) 449-2320.

Questions homeowners ask

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Newbury Park?

Yes. The 24/7 line is answered every day, including nights, weekends and holidays. Same-day response is offered when available. Call and tell us where the water is and what is wet.

What should I do before the crew arrives?

Shut off the water at the fixture or the main if you can. Keep people and pets out of the wet area, lift small items off wet floors and photograph the damage. Leave power off in wet rooms unless the panel is completely dry to reach.

How do you decide what gets dried and what gets removed?

Moisture readings and the water category decide. Clean-water drywall and framing can often be dried in place. Soaked pad, wet insulation and board that has gone soft are usually removed. The water mitigation page explains the drying side in detail.

Will my homeowner policy pay for water damage?

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, and gradual leaks and flood damage often are not. Check your policy or ask your insurer. Photos, a written scope and a drying log help whatever the answer is.

Do you work outside Newbury Park?

Yes. We serve Thousand Oaks, Camarillo, Westlake Village, Moorpark and Oak Park, along with the Newbury Park neighborhoods.
