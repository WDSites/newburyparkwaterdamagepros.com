What we do

Standards we work from

The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration and S520 standard for mold remediation are the industry references for this work. We name them so you can hold any company to the same yardstick, including us. Ask whether the crew at your door works to them, and ask how they decide when a structure is dry.

In practice that means a job is mapped before it is dried, monitored while it dries and checked before anything is closed up. A wall that feels dry to the hand can still read wet inside the stud bay, which is why readings, not touch, decide when drying is done.

What we will and will not tell you

We tell you what is wet, what can be dried and what has to be removed. We explain scope and price before work starts. We do not make health claims about mold or diagnose symptoms, since those questions belong to your physician. We do not quote a number from a phone description alone, because the real number depends on what the readings show.

We also say so when the cause of the problem belongs to another trade. A roof that keeps leaking needs a roofer, a failing supply line needs a plumber and a yard that floods the foundation needs a drainage contractor. Restoration puts the building right after the water, and it works best when the cause is fixed too.

Homes we work in

Newbury Park is a mix of housing from different eras. Casa Conejo was the first planned community here, Ventu Park began as a 1940s development, and Dos Vientos Ranch and Rancho Conejo Village are the newer residential areas. Each era has its own plumbing, its own wall materials and its own way of failing, so the first step on any job is to read the building before deciding what to open.

A typical job starts with a leak that someone notices late. A stain appears on a ceiling, a baseboard swells, a floor cups, or a closet smells damp. By the time it shows, water has usually traveled farther than the visible spot, which is why the first visit is about mapping where it went.

After the water is out, the work changes from speed to precision. Readings are taken daily, equipment is moved as the wet zone shrinks, and nothing is closed up until the numbers say the structure is dry. That patience is what keeps a repaired wall from turning into a mold problem six months later.

Working with your insurance

Insurance varies by policy, so check your policy or ask your insurer. In general terms, sudden and accidental water damage is often covered and gradual leaks often are not. Flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Photos, a written scope and a daily drying log help whatever the outcome, and we keep that documentation as part of every job.

What to expect on a call
