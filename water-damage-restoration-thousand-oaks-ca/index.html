Where water trouble starts across Thousand Oaks

Thousand Oaks is a big city, and the water problem you have depends on which part you live in. The central and eastern neighborhoods sit a short run east of Newbury Park on US 101, while the hillsides climb north into the Simi Hills and south into the Santa Monica Mountains. Each area fails in its own way.

Central tracts

The large subdivisions that the Janss family developed after buying about 10,000 acres are now long-settled neighborhoods. Expect supply lines, water heaters and washer hoses to be the usual culprits, so know where your shutoff valve is.

Hillside streets

Steep lots on Mount Clef Ridge and the southern slopes send water downhill fast. A leak on an upper level can reach a lower floor or a garage before anyone sees a stain.

Hidden Valley edge

Hidden Valley sits in the southernmost part of the city, with a rural feel. Irrigation lines, outbuildings and long driveways mean a leak can run unnoticed for days.

Wildwood side

Homes near 1,765-acre Wildwood Regional Park face roof and gutter loading in winter storms.

Burn-area ash and smoke

The Woolsey Fire of November 2018 burned almost 100,000 acres across Ventura and Los Angeles counties. Ash settled into attics and ducts, and fire-fighting water soaked what the flames missed.

If water is still spreading

Find the shutoff

Close the main valve at the house or the fixture valve under the sink or toilet. Your water bill or the city's Find Your Water Provider map tells you who to call if the street side is the problem.

Cut power only if dry

If water is near outlets or the panel, stay out. Switch off breakers only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Call the electric utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before calling us.

Keep it out of the street

City ordinance prohibits dumping anything other than clean water into storm drains. Do not hose or pump dirty water toward the curb.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, electronics and cushions to dry rooms. On a sloped lot, check lower rooms and the garage too.

Photograph everything

Take photos and video of the source and the damage before cleanup begins. Keep receipts for your insurer.

Services in Thousand Oaks, ordered by demand

Most calls here start with water mitigation. That means extraction units to pull standing water, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers to dry the structure, and moisture meters to confirm readings. We work to ANSI/IICRC S500 practice, and you can ask any company whether its crews do.

Next comes water damage cleanup for wet contents, antimicrobial treatment and removal of materials that cannot be saved. HEPA air scrubbers and negative air containment keep dust out of clean rooms.

Once the structure is dry, water damage repair rebuilds drywall, baseboards and flooring. Where mold appears, mold remediation follows ANSI/IICRC S520 practice. Mold needs moisture, so the water source gets fixed first.

Ash and smoke aftermath of the 2018 fires

The Woolsey Fire is a smoke and ash story, not a flood story. Residue lingered in attics, HVAC ducts and wall cavities, and crews hosing down hot spots left water behind. A ceiling stain or musty smell near the old burn area is a moisture problem, and a thermal imaging camera finds the wet material.

Cost and insurance

Cost depends on how much water, how long it sat, what the water carried and which materials it reached. Scope and price are explained before work starts.

Standard homeowners' insurance does not cover flood damage. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep photos, video, a written scope and receipts.

Permits and utilities in Thousand Oaks

The City of Thousand Oaks building division handles permits through the TO/24 portal, and inspections can only be scheduled there. Replacing a water heater or plumbing fixtures, and repairing gas, water or sewer lines, are on the city permit list. Permit rules vary by scope, so call Building at (805) 449-2320 before repair work.

For water service questions, the city bills at (805) 449-2201 and answers other questions at (805) 449-2100. Four purveyors serve the valley, including City of Thousand Oaks, California American Water, California Water Service and Camrosa Water District. Neighbors in Camarillo or Westlake Village deal with different local offices.
