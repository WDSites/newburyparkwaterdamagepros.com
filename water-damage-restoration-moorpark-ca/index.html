Two Moorpark housing worlds, two ways water shows up

State Route 118 (Los Angeles Avenue) cuts Moorpark in two. North of it, Old Town and High Street grew up around the rail-era townsite, where pepper trees line the historic center and the Moorpark train station still stands. Buildings here are often older, so original supply lines, drains and roof framing may still be in service. Have a plumber identify your pipe material before deciding how far a repair should go.

South of 118 and south of the Arroyo Simi, Peach Hill and Mountain Meadows are newer tract neighborhoods. Newer pipe still leaks. An upstairs toilet supply line, a washer valve or a garage water heater can soak a ceiling for hours unnoticed. The wet drywall is often a floor below the failure, so we trace it with a moisture meter and thermal imaging camera.

Water spreading in a Moorpark home is an emergency

The clock starts the moment water leaves the pipe. Drywall, insulation and subfloor soak it up within hours, and the longer they stay wet the more has to be cut out. Call early, even if you are still stopping the leak.

If water is still spreading

Stop the source

Close the fixture shutoff, or the main valve where your house meets the street line. Older Old Town homes may have a stiff valve, so find it before you need it.

Keep clear of electricity

If water reaches outlets or the panel, stay out. Cut power only if the panel is reachable dry, and call the utility or 911 about any electrical or gas risk before calling us.

Move what you can

Lift rugs, electronics and boxes off wet floors, and pull furniture away from wet walls. Leave soaked drywall and ceilings alone, since they can drop.

Photograph everything

Take photos and video of the source, the wet areas and damaged items before anything moves. Keep receipts for any repairs or supplies.

Skip the storm drain

Do not hose or pump sewage or dirty water into the street. Call the restoration line at (805) 291-8940 and we will set up extraction units to take it out safely.

What the valley floor and the weather add to the risk

Arroyo Simi valley

Central Moorpark sits at about 515 feet in a valley the Arroyo Simi created. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address before you assume a low spot is safe.

Winter-weighted rain

The city averages about 17.4 inches of precipitation a year, mostly in winter. A roof leak or clogged gutter often shows up after the first heavy storm of the season.

Older Old Town buildings

The oldest structures near High Street may have original plumbing and roofing. Hidden leaks inside walls can run for weeks before a stain appears.

Flood is not covered

Standard homeowners policies generally exclude flood. Rain through a roof and a burst pipe are different claims, so ask your insurer which applies.

Mold needs moisture, so a small leak left damp can feed it. We fix the water first and follow ANSI/IICRC S520 practice for any mold work.

Services we offer in Moorpark, in order of demand

Most calls here start with a hidden leak or a failed appliance, so water mitigation comes first. That means extraction, then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers with daily moisture readings until the structure reaches dry standards. We work to ANSI/IICRC S500 practice, and you can ask any company whether its crews do.

Once the building is dry, water damage repair replaces drywall, insulation, baseboards and flooring, and a plumber fixes the failed line. When the water was fast and heavy, emergency water removal gets standing water out before it reaches more rooms. For rain or runoff that comes in from outside, flood damage restoration covers cleanup, disinfection and drying of what the water touched.
