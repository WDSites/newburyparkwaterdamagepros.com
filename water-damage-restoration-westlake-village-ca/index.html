Where water goes wrong in a lake-and-golf-course town

Westlake Village was built around Westlake Lake, and the streets near it are full of irrigation. Lawns, planters and slopes along the lake and the three golf courses (Westlake Golf Course, Sherwood Country Club and North Ranch Country Club) run on buried lines. A cracked line or a stuck valve can soak a foundation edge or push water into a crawl space for days before anyone sees it.

Irrigation and lawn lines

Backflow or a broken sprinkler line near a wall can feed water under flooring. If a baseboard is damp on a dry day, check the yard side first.

Supply lines and appliances

Homes with hardwood and stone floors hide small leaks well. A dishwasher, ice maker or washer hose can wet the subfloor long before the surface shows it, and cupped planks are a late sign.

Slab and in-wall leaks

A hot spot on a tile floor, a hum from a wall or a water meter that moves with every tap closed points to a hidden pressurized line.

Creek-side drainage

Triunfo Creek runs near the community, so lots along it deserve a look after heavy rain. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address.

The Woolsey Fire in November 2018 also left a mark. Burned slopes shed water differently, so grading and drain outlets near hillside homes are worth checking after a storm.

Water is spreading in your Westlake Village home: what to do first

Speed matters because water travels. On a hardwood or stone-and-tile floor it moves sideways under the finish and wicks up drywall. Stop the source, then protect people, then call.

If water is still spreading

Shut the water off

Close the fixture valve or the main shutoff. For irrigation, shut the backflow or zone valve and turn off the controller.

Mind the electricity

Stay out of standing water near outlets or the panel. Cut power only if the panel can be reached dry. For any electrical or gas risk, call the utility or 911 before the restoration line.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, books and small furniture off wet hardwood. Do not stack items on wet carpet, and leave heavy stone pieces alone.

Photograph everything

Take photos and video of the source, the wet surfaces and damaged contents before any cleanup, for your insurer.

Keep contaminated water out of drains

Do not hose sewage or dirty water toward the street or a storm drain. Call us at (805) 291-8940 and we will explain the next step.

What drives cost, and what to ask first

Cost depends on how much area is wet, how long it sat, whether the water was clean or contaminated, and what finishes are involved. Stone, engineered wood and custom cabinetry take more care to dry and more to replace. Scope and price are explained before work starts.

Permit rules vary by scope, so ask the City of Westlake Village building department before repair work. Replacing plumbing lines or fixtures is the kind of work cities often regulate. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by insurance, while gradual leaks often are not, so check your policy or ask your insurer. Standard homeowners' insurance does not cover flood.

Mold needs moisture, so fix the water source first. If growth appears, mold remediation follows ANSI/IICRC S520 practice, and health questions belong to a physician.

Does the county line change anything for my project?

Yes. Westlake Village is in Los Angeles County, so permits go through the City of Westlake Village building department, not Ventura County or Thousand Oaks. Ask them before repair work, since permit rules vary by scope.

They can. We do not know your association's rules, so ask your association before work starts about approvals, contractor requirements or access hours. Tell us what you learn and we will plan around it.
