Late-1960s tract homes and the plumbing behind the walls

Homes in Oak Park began going up in the late 1960s, built by Metropolitan Development Corporation on part of Rancho Simi. Depending on what past owners replaced, a house from that era may still have original supply lines, an aging water heater or older roof sections. Nobody can tell from the curb, so we sweep walls and ceilings with a moisture meter and thermal imaging camera first.

Water spreading through your Oak Park home? Do this first

Water in a two-story tract home travels fast, from an upstairs bathroom through the ceiling below and into the walls. Work through these steps first.

If water is still spreading

Close the main valve

Find the shutoff where the water line enters the house and turn it fully. If a single fixture is leaking, its own stop valve under the sink or behind the toilet may be enough.

Mind the panel

If water reaches outlets or the electrical panel, stay out. Cut power only if you can reach the panel dry, and call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before calling us.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, small furniture and electronics off wet floors. Photograph everything before you move it.

Keep it out of the street

Do not pump or hose dirty water toward a storm drain. Sewage that backs up from a floor drain stays inside until trained crews extract it.

Document, then call

Take photos and video of the source and the damage for your insurer. Then call (805) 291-8940 and tell us which rooms are affected.

Sewage backups

Oak Park wastewater is treated at the Tapia Water Reclamation Facility, run by the Triunfo County Sanitation District. If sewage rises in several drains at once, call the district about the main line. If only your home is affected, a plumber clears the line, and we handle extraction and disinfection of the affected area.

Services in the order Oak Park homes tend to need them

Most calls here start with a slow or hidden leak, so water mitigation comes first. That means extraction, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, with daily readings until framing is dry.

Mold follows when a leak sits unnoticed behind a vanity or in a closet wall. Mold remediation uses containment, negative air and HEPA air scrubbers, following ANSI/IICRC S520 practice, and the water source has to be fixed first. Questions about health belong to a physician.

When a supply line lets go or a dishwasher floods the kitchen, emergency water removal with extraction units comes ahead of everything else. Once dry, water damage repair replaces drywall, baseboards and flooring.

What affects cost, and who to ask about permits

Price depends on how many rooms are wet, how long the water sat, what the water carried and which materials absorbed it. We explain scope and price before work starts.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowners' insurance, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Standard policies do not cover flood. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep photos, video and receipts.

Because Oak Park is unincorporated, permit rules vary by scope and go through Ventura County, not a city office. Ask county building and safety before repair work. Neighbors in Westlake Village deal with a city department instead, and Thousand Oaks has its own TO/24 process, so do not assume the rules match.

FAQs from Oak Park

Do you cover every ZIP code in Oak Park?

Oak Park is one ZIP, 91377, which covers the community in the Simi Hills east of Thousand Oaks along the Los Angeles County line.

The USPS assigned 91377 in 1999. Before that the community shared 91301 with Agoura Hills, which is why some mail, records and older listings still say Agoura Hills.

Is emergency water removal available in Oak Park?

Yes. Emergency dispatch runs around the clock, and same-day response is offered when available. Call (805) 291-8940, tell us where the water is coming from, and shut off the main valve if you can reach it safely.

Who issues permits for repairs in Oak Park?

Oak Park is an unincorporated community, so there is no city building department. Permits go through Ventura County building and safety. Rules vary by scope, so ask the county before repair work begins.

Can creek-side drainage cause water damage inside my home?

It can. Homes near Medea Creek or Lindero Creek may see saturated soil and runoff at slab edges after heavy rain. Check FEMA's Flood Map Service Center for your address, and ask your insurer, since standard homeowners' policies do not cover flood.

Do you work in the Oak Park Unified School District area?

Yes. Oak Park Unified, which separated from Simi Valley Unified in 1977, covers the same community as ZIP 91377. Homes near Medea Creek Middle School and Oak Park High School are in our service area.

You are based in Newbury Park, so how do you reach Oak Park?

Crews travel east from Newbury Park, past Thousand Oaks, to the Los Angeles County line. Same-day response is offered when available,.

Do you clean up after a nearby fire?

Yes. Fire years such as 1982 and the Woolsey Fire of November 2018 left ash and smoke residue, and hose water can soak walls and insulation. We dry the structure and remove residue, and we do not treat it as a flood loss.
